Chapter 01

Getting Started

Your Chromebook is a learning tool issued by the school. It runs Chrome OS, a lightweight operating system built by Google. Unlike a Windows PC or Mac, most of what you do on a Chromebook lives in the cloud — meaning your work is saved automatically and you can access it from any device when you log in with your school Google account.

Logging In

Use your school-issued Google account (usually firstname.lastname@district.org). Never use a personal Google account on a school Chromebook. If you forget your password, ask your teacher or visit the front office.

The Shelf

The bar at the bottom of your screen is called the Shelf. It holds pinned apps, your clock, Wi-Fi status, and battery level. Click the circle icon (Launcher) on the far left to search for apps and files.

The Launcher

Press the Search/Launcher key (the magnifying glass key, where Caps Lock usually is) to open the Launcher. From here you can search your apps, Google Drive, and the web all at once.

Quick Settings

Click the time in the bottom-right corner to open Quick Settings. From here, adjust Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, volume, screen brightness, and check your battery percentage.

The Chromebook Keyboard — What's Different?

Chromebook keyboards look slightly different from what you might be used to. Here are the keys unique to Chrome OS:

💡 Did You Know?

There is no traditional Delete key on a Chromebook. To delete text to the right of your cursor, press Alt + Backspace.

Changing Your Keyboard Language

Need to type in Spanish, French, or another language? Chrome OS makes it easy to switch between keyboard input methods.

  1. Click the time in the bottom-right corner to open Quick Settings, then click the gear icon to open Settings.
  2. On the left sidebar, click Advanced, then select Languages and inputs.
  3. Under Input methods, click Add input methods and search for the language you need (e.g., "Spanish — Latin America").
  4. Once added, you'll see a language switcher appear on the Shelf. Click it or press Ctrl + Space to toggle between languages instantly.
🌎 International Students

If your home language uses a different script (e.g., Chinese, Arabic, Korean), you can add those input methods too. Some may require enabling a special IME (Input Method Editor) which will appear as an icon on your shelf.

Chapter 02

Accessibility & ChromeVOX

Chrome OS includes powerful built-in accessibility tools designed to make technology work for every learner. These tools are free, built-in, and require no special software to install.

ChromeVOX — The Built-In Screen Reader

ChromeVOX is Google's screen reader for Chrome OS. It reads aloud everything on your screen — web pages, menus, buttons, and documents — using text-to-speech. It's designed for students who are blind, have low vision, or benefit from hearing content read to them.

Turning ChromeVOX On or Off

  • Press Ctrl + Alt + Z to toggle ChromeVOX on or off at any time.
  • When enabled, you'll hear a chime and ChromeVOX will announce items as you navigate with your keyboard.

Other Accessibility Features

🔍

Screen Magnifier

Press Ctrl + Search + M to turn on the full-screen magnifier. Use Ctrl + Alt + Brightness Up/Down to zoom in and out while it's active.

🎨

High Contrast Mode

Press Ctrl + Search + H to toggle high contrast mode, which inverts colors and makes text easier to read for students with visual sensitivities.

🖱️

Large Cursor & Carets

In Settings → Accessibility, you can enable a larger mouse cursor and a text cursor (caret) that highlights where you're typing — helpful for staying focused on your work.

💬

Dictation (Voice Typing)

Press Search + D to start dictating. Speak normally and Chrome OS will type for you. Works in any text field — great for longer writing assignments.

🖊️

Select-to-Speak

In Accessibility settings, enable Select-to-Speak. Then hold Search and click on any text — or highlight text and press Search + S — to have it read aloud.

⌨️

On-Screen Keyboard

Enable the on-screen keyboard in Accessibility settings for touch-based typing. Useful if a physical key is broken or for students who prefer tap-to-type input.

Chapter 04

Special Features & Built-In Apps

Your Chromebook comes with more built-in tools than most students realize. Here's a tour of the most useful ones.

📁

Files App

Click the Files app on your shelf to browse your local storage and Google Drive. Anything saved to Google Drive is available on any device. Avoid saving important work only to Downloads — that storage is local and could be lost.

📸

Camera

The built-in Camera app lets you take photos, record video, or scan QR codes. Switch to Document mode to capture flat images of papers or whiteboards — great for saving handwritten notes.

🖩

Calculator

Chrome OS has a built-in calculator with standard, scientific, and unit converter modes. Search "Calculator" in the Launcher to open it without loading a website.

📋

Clipboard Manager

Press Search + V to open your clipboard history. Chrome OS keeps the last five items you copied, so you can paste something you copied earlier without having to find it again.

🪟

Split Screen

To work in two windows at once, drag a window to the left or right edge of the screen until it snaps into place. Then select a second window to fill the other half. This is perfect for researching and writing at the same time.

🔒

Guest Mode

If a family member needs to use your Chromebook, use Guest Mode from the login screen. Guest sessions don't save any data and won't touch your school account. However, school-managed Chromebooks may have Guest Mode disabled.

🌐

Offline Mode

Google Docs, Slides, and Sheets all work offline if you enable it. In Google Drive, right-click a file and toggle Available Offline. Changes sync automatically when you reconnect to Wi-Fi.

🎯

Virtual Desks

Press Search + Shift + = to create a new virtual desktop. Use separate desks for different classes — keep your math apps on Desk 1 and your English apps on Desk 2. Switch with Search + [ or ].

🤖

Google Assistant / Gemini

Some Chromebooks support Google Assistant or Gemini AI. You can ask questions, set reminders, or get quick help. Check with your teacher before using AI tools on assignments — Roosevelt has guidelines on appropriate use.

Chapter 05

Google Workspace — The Full Breakdown

Google Workspace (sometimes called G Suite) is the set of tools you'll use for nearly every assignment at Roosevelt. Here's what each app does and how to use it well.

Docs

Google Docs

Your word processor for essays, reports, and writing assignments.

Getting Started

  • Go to docs.google.com or open it from Google Drive.
  • Click the + button (Blank) to start a new document.
  • Name your document right away by clicking Untitled document at the top.
  • All changes save automatically — look for Saved to Drive in the top bar.

Formatting Basics

  • Use the Styles dropdown (usually shows "Normal text") to apply Heading 1, Heading 2, etc. — this builds a document outline automatically.
  • Ctrl+B = Bold, Ctrl+I = Italic, Ctrl+U = Underline
  • Use Format → Line & paragraph spacing to set double spacing for essays.
  • Insert images with Insert → Image. You can search the web directly inside Docs.

Collaboration & Sharing

  • Click the blue Share button to share with your teacher or a classmate.
  • Set permission to Viewer, Commenter, or Editor depending on what you want them to do.
  • To leave a comment, highlight text and press Ctrl+Alt+M — perfect for peer review.
  • Click See revision history under File → Version history to see every change ever made and who made it.

Power Features

  • Voice typing: Tools → Voice typing. Speak and it types for you.
  • Explore: Tools → Explore. Research and insert citations without leaving the doc.
  • Word count: Tools → Word count (Ctrl+Shift+C).
  • Dictionary: Highlight a word and go to Tools → Dictionary for definitions.
📝 Assignment Tip

When your teacher assigns a Google Doc through Google Classroom, a personal copy is automatically created in your Drive. Never type directly in a shared template — always open your own copy from Classroom.

Slides

Google Slides

Build presentations for class projects, reports, and group work.

Creating & Designing

  • Choose a Theme from the right panel when you first open Slides — it sets consistent fonts and colors automatically.
  • Click on any text box to edit it. Click outside to deselect.
  • Add new slides with Ctrl+M or right-click a slide in the panel and choose New slide.
  • Use Slide → Change layout to pick different arrangements (title, title & body, blank, etc.).

Adding Media

  • Insert → Image to add photos (from Drive, web, or your camera).
  • Insert → Video to embed YouTube videos directly into a slide.
  • Insert → Link or Ctrl+K to add a hyperlink to text or an image.

Presenting

  • Press the Present button (top right) or hit Ctrl+F5 to start presenting.
  • Use → or Space to advance, ← to go back during a presentation.
  • Press B to blank the screen if you want the audience to focus on you, not the slide.
  • Add Speaker Notes at the bottom of each slide — only you see them during presentation mode.

Transitions & Animations

  • Go to Slide → Transition to add an animation between slides.
  • Click an object, then Insert → Animation to animate individual elements like text or images.
  • Keep animations simple — too many can distract from your content.
Sheets

Google Sheets

Organize data, do calculations, and build charts.

The Basics

  • A spreadsheet is a grid of cells. Each cell has an address like A1 (column A, row 1).
  • Click a cell and type to enter data. Press Enter to move down, Tab to move right.
  • To resize a column, drag the border between column letters at the top.
  • Format numbers as currency, percentages, or dates using Format → Number.

Essential Formulas

  • =SUM(A1:A10) — Adds up a range of numbers
  • =AVERAGE(B1:B5) — Calculates the average
  • =MAX(C1:C20) / =MIN(...) — Finds highest or lowest value
  • =COUNT(D1:D10) — Counts how many cells have numbers
  • All formulas start with =

Charts & Graphs

  • Select your data, then go to Insert → Chart.
  • Sheets will suggest a chart type. You can change it in the Chart editor panel on the right.
  • Double-click the chart to edit its title, colors, and labels.

Useful Features

  • Freeze rows/columns: View → Freeze to keep headers visible while scrolling.
  • Sort & Filter: Data → Sort range or Create a filter to organize your data.
  • Conditional formatting: Format → Conditional formatting to automatically color cells based on values.
Forms

Google Forms

Take quizzes, submit surveys, and complete digital assignments.

Filling Out a Form

  • Your teacher will share a link — click it and the form opens in your browser.
  • Answer each question and click Next (if it's multiple pages) or Submit when done.
  • Look for a confirmation message after submitting — that means it went through.
  • Some forms let you Edit your response after submitting if you need to fix something.

When You're Creating a Form (Projects)

  • Go to forms.google.com and click the + to start a blank form.
  • Click the question title to edit it. Use the dropdown on the right to pick the question type: Multiple choice, Checkboxes, Short answer, Paragraph, etc.
  • Toggle Required to make questions mandatory.
  • Click the bar chart icon to see responses after people submit.
Sites

Google Sites

Build your own websites for class projects and portfolios.

Building a Site

  • Go to sites.google.com and click New Site.
  • Click on any element to edit it. Drag sections to rearrange them.
  • Use the right panel to insert: text boxes, images, videos, Google Docs/Slides/Sheets, and more.
  • Add new pages using the Pages tab on the right sidebar.

Themes & Publishing

  • Click Themes in the right panel to choose colors and fonts for your entire site at once.
  • Preview your site with the eye icon (👁️) in the top bar.
  • When ready, click Publish. You can set the site to be visible to your school only, or to the public.
  • Copy the published link to share with your teacher.
Drawings

Google Drawings

Create diagrams, graphic organizers, infographics, and visual projects.

What You Can Do

  • Access Google Drawings at docs.google.com/drawings.
  • Insert shapes, text boxes, lines, arrows, and images onto a blank canvas.
  • Use it to make concept maps, timelines, flowcharts, and graphic organizers for any subject.
  • Change colors, line thickness, and fill styles from the toolbar.

Tips for Projects

  • Set a custom canvas size under File → Page setup for posters or specific dimensions.
  • Use Arrange → Align to line up objects neatly.
  • Download your drawing as PNG or PDF via File → Download to insert it into Docs or Slides.
  • Group objects (Ctrl+G) so they move together as one piece.
Chapter 06

Student Websites & Platforms

Roosevelt uses a number of online platforms to support your learning. Here's what each one is and how to use it.

C

Clever

clever.com

Clever is your single sign-on portal — a dashboard that connects to almost every learning app the school uses. Instead of remembering separate passwords for each tool, you log into Clever with your school Google account and click the app you need. If an app isn't launching, try refreshing Clever or clearing your browser cookies.

Log in using your school Google account. If you see a "Login with Google" button, use that.
GC

Google Classroom

classroom.google.com

Google Classroom is where your teachers post assignments, announcements, and resources. When you open it, you'll see all your enrolled classes. Click a class to see the Stream (announcements), Classwork (assignments and materials), and People (your class roster). Click Turn In on assignments to submit your work — never email your work unless your teacher specifically asks.

Check Classroom every morning and after school. Teachers post reminders and last-minute instructions there.
M

Gmail (School Email)

mail.google.com

Your school Google account comes with a Gmail inbox. Use it to communicate professionally with teachers, counselors, and staff. Always include a clear subject line, use complete sentences, sign your name, and include your class period when emailing a teacher. Do not use school email for personal messages or to sign up for non-school websites.

Sample subject line: "Period 3 — Question about Tuesday's essay assignment"
KA

Khan Academy

khanacademy.org

Khan Academy offers free video lessons and practice exercises for every subject — math, science, history, English, SAT prep, and more. When your teacher assigns work on Khan Academy, it appears in your personal dashboard. You can also explore any topic on your own at any time. The platform tracks your progress and awards energy points to keep you motivated.

Use Khan Academy the night before a test to review topics you feel unsure about.
SC

Student Connect / Aeries

Check with your school for the link

Student Connect (powered by Aeries) is your student information portal. Log in to check your grades, attendance record, class schedule, and report cards. Your parents/guardians have their own login to view the same information. If you notice a grade that looks incorrect, contact your teacher directly — do not wait until the end of the semester.

Set a habit of checking Student Connect every Friday to stay on top of missing assignments before they pile up.
TCI

TCI (Teachers' Curriculum Institute)

teachtci.com

TCI is a digital textbook and curriculum platform used primarily in Social Studies and Science classes at many schools. Your teacher will give you a class code to join. Inside TCI, you'll find your digital textbook chapters, interactive activities, note-taking tools, and assignments. The platform works best in Chrome — always access it on your Chromebook for the smoothest experience.

Use the TCI read-aloud feature (headphones icon) if you want to have the textbook read to you while you follow along.
Chapter 07

Care, Cleaning & Responsibility

Your Chromebook is school property. Treating it with care protects your learning and avoids damage fees. These habits take seconds but can prevent costly repairs.

✅ Do This

  • Carry your Chromebook with two hands, closed, with the screen facing your body.
  • Store it in its protective sleeve or case when not in use.
  • Charge it overnight so it's fully charged for school every morning.
  • Keep it on a flat, stable surface when in use.
  • Clean the screen gently with a dry or lightly damp microfiber cloth.
  • Clean the keyboard with a dry microfiber cloth or soft brush.
  • Report any cracks, damage, or missing keys to your teacher immediately.
  • Log out when done — especially on a shared device.

❌ Don't Do This

  • Never put heavy objects on top of a closed Chromebook.
  • Never eat or drink near your Chromebook — liquids destroy keyboards instantly.
  • Never leave it in a hot car or in direct sunlight for extended periods.
  • Never use paper towels, tissues, or rough cloths to clean the screen.
  • Never use cleaning sprays or alcohol-based products on the screen.
  • Never pick up a Chromebook by the screen — always pick it up by the base.
  • Never force the screen back further than it naturally goes.
  • Never share your login credentials with anyone.

Charging Your Chromebook

🔋

Nightly Charging

Plug in your Chromebook every night. Most Chromebook batteries last 8–10 hours, so a full overnight charge should power you through the entire school day.

🔌

Use the Right Charger

Only use the charger that came with your Chromebook. Using the wrong charger can damage the battery over time. If you lose the charger, report it to your school right away.

⚠️

Low Battery Warning

When battery drops below 15%, save your work immediately and find a charging opportunity. Chrome OS will warn you at 15% and again at 5%. Don't ignore these warnings.

🏫 School Policy Reminder

Students who damage school Chromebooks through negligence may be responsible for repair costs. If your Chromebook is stolen, lost, or damaged by someone else, report it to the front office as soon as possible. Acting quickly makes a difference in how the situation is handled.
